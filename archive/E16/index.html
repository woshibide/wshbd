Pyotr Goloub is a computational designer, blurring the line between design and engineering. As design evolves from fixed environments into adaptable systems, he builds his own tooling to reshape the process itself, removing friction between idea and execution. This approach accelerates both his own speed and internal team workflows, without compromising precision or visual quality. Fluent across both creative and technical domains, he understands the underlying mechanics that connect them, allowing him to design with systems, code, and a clear awareness of how implementation shape material and ultimately the outcomes.

Book

Netherlands

2024 Dec

An almost 1,000-page book of things that match the vibe of chairs

I used public-domain images from the Art Institute of Chicago and the Metropolitan Museum of Art to make this self-initiated book of 1,500 images. Each spread pairs a chair with another image, asking what meaning can emerge between the two. Code helped streamline many parts of production, including a custom Processing app I made to compare images and choose pairings that looked or felt right together. The tool supported visual judgement, with the book giving those choices a repeated form across its spreads. A second main outcome is bookmaking, a collection of scripts and tools for producing books
