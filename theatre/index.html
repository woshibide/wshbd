Pyotr Goloub is a computational designer, blurring the line between design and engineering. As design evolves from fixed environments into adaptable systems, he builds his own tooling to reshape the process itself, removing friction between idea and execution. This approach accelerates both his own speed and internal team workflows, without compromising precision or visual quality. Fluent across both creative and technical domains, he understands the underlying mechanics that connect them, allowing him to design with systems, code, and a clear awareness of how implementation shape material and ultimately the outcomes.

Site-specific photobookThe book connects sculpture, photography and the printed spread in a layered sequence. Transparent paper and light connect those layers, allowing an image to be encountered through another surface. The sequence works through the physical act of turning pages, with the composition changing as material is revealed or covered. The design gives this non-linear approach a book form. Its relationship with cinema lies in that unfolding experience, where the image is encountered through movement and time

Web tool for styling dendrogramsAt Rewire 2025, our team was producing a zine for Rewire Reflections when Taliesin Herb, one of the writers, needed a dendrogram to accompany his contribution. The diagram had to fit Rewire’s visual language and work on the printed page. A custom web tool made it possible to adjust nodes, labels, line widths and colours, then export the result as an SVG. It connected Taliesin’s data analysis with the zine’s production, giving the team a way to prepare and style the diagram for publication

Identity and camera work for a vineyard in the middle of the cityDe Haagse Stadswijngaard asked for a new wine label for a batch of 500 bottles. The response grew into a wider identity built around slowness and sincerity. An almost 1,000-page book of things that match the vibe of chairsI used public-domain images from the Art Institute of Chicago and the Metropolitan Museum of Art to make this self-initiated book of 1,500 images. Each spread pairs a chair with another image, asking what meaning can emerge between the two. Code helped streamline many parts of production, including a custom Processing app I made to compare images and choose pairings that looked or felt right together. The tool supported visual judgement, with the book giving those choices a repeated form across its spreads. A second main outcome is bookmaking, a collection of scripts and tools for producing books

Visual essay born out of research into the Best Dutch Design Book archive at Museum MeermanoFlying Over The Dutch Nest takes the Best Dutch Design Book collection as a starting point for looking at how ideas and appearances accumulate around art academies. The publication brings that inquiry into a sequence of visual material, leaving the images room to be encountered without explanatory captions. Its cover includes a rectangular opening, making looking through the book part of handling it. The work gives “eclectic opportunism” a visual setting through collection, framing and sequence, while the relationships between its images remain open to the reader

Painting-exhibition poster seriesThe posters use a paint tube as the setting for the exhibition information. Marks taken from the exhibiting artists’ canvases appear on the tubes, bringing elements of the paintings into the announcement with the artists’ approval. Apparel

IMAGE 1 OUT OF 2

2024 Jul

Happy hour. May your coffee be sweet. Forward momentum, clarity of purpose. Good luck in business endeavours and skill in decision-making. Strategic moves, optimised processesI combined two visual cultures on one shirt: the office worker’s collared uniform and imagery associated with Russian prisoners’ tattoos. Blue ballpoint-style drawings cover the pale blue fabric, turning its familiar working-day form into the setting for another set of signs. The work follows the kind of visual pairing I associate with Vladimir Tsesler, where bringing two things together can produce a third meaning. The resulting garment carries that collision on the body, with the shirt and its marks remaining recognisable as different sources
